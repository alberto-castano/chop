# Code of Conduct

Chop welcomes people with different backgrounds, identities, abilities, and levels of experience.
Everyone should be able to ask questions, contribute, and disagree without harassment or intimidation.

## Expected behavior

- Treat people with respect. Critique ideas and code without attacking the person.
- Give clear, constructive feedback. Explain the problem and suggest a way forward when possible.
- Listen to corrections and take responsibility for mistakes.
- Respect people's time, boundaries, and privacy.
- Help keep discussions relevant to the project.

Technical disagreement is welcome. Personal attacks are not.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, threats, intimidation, or discriminatory abuse.
- Sexual harassment, unwanted sexual attention, or sexualized content directed at participants.
- Insults, deliberate humiliation, or repeated disruption of discussions.
- Publishing private information without permission, including private correspondence.
- Continuing unwanted contact after someone asks you to stop.
- Retaliation against someone who reports a concern or helps with a review.

## Scope

This policy applies to project issues, pull requests, discussions, and other community spaces maintained by Chop.
It also applies when someone represents the project in public and to conduct connected to project participation.
Contributors and maintainers follow the same rules.

## Report a concern

**Private reporting contact: awaiting maintainer confirmation.**

Report concerns privately. Include links, dates, a description of what happened, and any relevant evidence you can safely share.
Tell the maintainer if you have concerns about further contact or disclosure.
Do not post personal details or sensitive evidence in a public issue.

For software vulnerabilities, use the separate reporting channel in [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md).

## How maintainers respond

Maintainers review reports in context and give the people involved a chance to respond when appropriate.
They limit disclosure of reports to what is needed to investigate and respond. They cannot promise absolute confidentiality.

Depending on the severity and pattern of behavior, maintainers may:

- Ask someone to change their behavior or edit a contribution.
- Remove or lock content that violates this policy.
- Issue a warning or restrict participation for a stated period.
- Permanently exclude someone from project spaces.

Serious incidents can lead to immediate restrictions. Repeated violations can lead to stronger action.
Maintainers should explain the decision and any conditions for returning to the project when practical.

A maintainer involved in a report should not decide its outcome alone.
If no uninvolved maintainer is available, the project should seek an independent reviewer acceptable to the reporter.
Anyone affected by a decision can request reconsideration through the private reporting contact and provide additional context.
